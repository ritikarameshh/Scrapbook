Two Validators, Explained

How your AR app decides "yes, the user is pointing at the right thing."
The job of a validator: watch the camera and answer one question — does what the camera sees match the outline we're asking the user to align to? When the answer is yes, the app advances (drops the stamp). You have two very different ways of answering that question.
Validator A

Visual Edge Match

"Trace the shape with your eyes."
How it works
  1. Take the outline shape (SVG) and turn it into a black-and-white silhouette.
  2. 10× a second, grab the area of the camera inside the outline box.
  3. Run an edge detector on it (finds the dark/light borders — the "lines" in the scene).
  4. Compare the two: how much do the camera's edges overlap with the outline? More overlap = better score.
Pros
+
Works with any image you can draw an outline of — no special training file needed.
+
Cheap, runs entirely in the browser, no extra library.
+
You stay in control of the math — easy to tune thresholds.
Cons
−
Easily fooled by busy backgrounds (lots of edges that aren't your subject).
−
Sensitive to lighting and rotation — a tilted view scores worse even if it's correct.
−
No idea where the object actually is in 3D space.
Validator B

MindAR Image Tracking

"Did the tracker recognize the landmark?"
How it works
  1. Beforehand, you "train" a target file from a reference photo of the landmark.
  2. MindAR watches the camera and looks for that landmark's feature points (little fingerprints of texture).
  3. It fires an event: targetFound or targetLost.
  4. Your validator just checks that flag: visible = pass.
Pros
+
Far more accurate — it's actually recognizing the thing, not just shape overlap.
+
Gives you 3D position too, so you can anchor the stamp to the landmark.
+
Robust to angle, lighting, partial occlusion.
Cons
−
Needs a pre-compiled target file per landmark — extra prep work.
−
Only works on things with rich texture; flat / repetitive surfaces won't track.
−
Heavier — pulls in a tracking library and uses more CPU.
